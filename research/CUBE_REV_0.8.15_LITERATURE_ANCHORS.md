# CUBE-REV 0.8.15 Literature Anchors

## Official title

**CUBE-REV 0.8.15 — Cognitive Mechanism-axis Lattice, Strategy-transition Signatures & Identifiability-constrained Hypothesis Registry**

## Use rule

This document does not use prior literature as a label dictionary for participants. It uses the literature to define competing mechanisms, observable consequences, and measurement hazards. CUBE-REV promotes a psychological interpretation only after a behavioral signature is distinguishable under the actual instrument.

## Anchors

### 1. The Planning Horizon for Movement Sequences

- DOI: `10.1523/ENEURO.0085-21.2021`
- Relevant construct: finite action-planning horizon and practice-dependent extension of that horizon.
- CUBE-REV use: motivates depth-indexed action policies and the question of whether first choices differ on states where shallow and deeper lookahead disagree.
- Non-use: a choice matching the depth-3 heuristic is not proof that a participant consciously simulated three moves.

### 2. Repetition Costs in Sequence Chunking

- DOI: `10.3758/s13423-023-02338-7`
- Relevant construct: sequence chunks can facilitate execution while producing switching or repetition costs.
- CUBE-REV use: motivates separate axes for chunk retrieval, perseverative interference, and strategy switching.
- Non-use: repeated face choices in isolated first-action trials are not sufficient evidence of a stored motor chunk.

### 3. Visuospatial Working Memory Capacity Predicts the Organization of Acquired Explicit Motor Sequences

- DOI: `10.1152/jn.00006.2009`
- Relevant construct: visuospatial working-memory capacity can covary with learning rate and chunk organization in explicit motor-sequence learning.
- CUBE-REV use: motivates a capacity axis and, more importantly, a negative control in which distinct latent capacities implement the same observable policy.
- Non-use: first-action and latency traces alone cannot identify a capacity trait.

### 4. Ability and Nonability Predictors of Real-World Skill Acquisition: The Case of Rubik’s Cube Solving

- DOI: `10.3390/jintelligence11010018`
- Relevant construct: real-world Rubik's Cube learning can relate differently to fluid intelligence, working memory, and nonability variables depending on the task and training regime.
- CUBE-REV use: prevents treating one broad cognitive-capacity construct as the universal explanation of cube performance.
- Non-use: CUBE-REV does not infer intelligence or working-memory capacity from a single task trace.

### 5. Reactivity to Confidence Ratings: Evidence of Impaired Rule-learning

- DOI: `10.1007/s11409-025-09413-5`
- Relevant construct: repeated confidence judgments can alter learning or strategy rather than passively report it.
- CUBE-REV use: supports retaining global post-task confidence while refusing to add trialwise confidence probes in 0.8.15.
- Non-use: absence of trialwise ratings is not treated as absence of metacognition.

### 6. A Multistrategy Cognitive Diagnosis Model Incorporating Item Response Times Based on Strategy Selection Theories

- DOI: `10.3102/10769986231200469`
- Relevant construct: joint use of responses and response times to model strategy selection.
- CUBE-REV use: supports treating first action, item structure, schedule position, previous action, and latency as a joint signature rather than reading latency alone as planning depth.
- Non-use: the 0.8.15 nearest-centroid synthetic audit is not a validated cognitive-diagnosis estimator and does not estimate participant latent classes.

## Epistemic boundary

The literature supports candidate axes, not automatic participant labels. The 0.8.15 synthetic study asks a narrower question:

> If a mechanism generated the current 28-trial first-action and latency observables, would the current instrument contain enough information to distinguish it from specified alternatives?

A positive synthetic control means that the deliberately separated generators leave different signatures under this implementation. A negative control means that no analysis can recover a latent distinction that was intentionally made observationally identical. Neither result establishes which mechanism real participants use.
