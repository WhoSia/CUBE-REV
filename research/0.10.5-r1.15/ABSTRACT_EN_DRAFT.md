## Working title

**Behavioral Reconstruction Traces Are Not Self-Interpreting: Observation-Operator Failure, Transport, and Authority in Competitive Speedcubing**

## Abstract

Fine-grained behavioral traces appear to offer direct access to expert problem solving, but recorded actions become scientific measurements only after a mapping from trace to construct has been specified. We study this measurement problem using public competitive speedcubing reconstructions, where exact move algebra and cube states permit unusually strong falsification of candidate observation operators. Across a serial research lineage, locally shorter state-equivalent routes failed as a standalone detour marker; full-state movement away from annotated phase endpoints was common under ordinary algorithms and failed a matched familywise null; phase-specific quotient geometry improved representation but exposed method-specific transport failure; and Roux reconstructions required explicit treatment of phase completion, route coordinate gauge, and block-preservation semantics. We therefore developed a state-defined Roux ontology in which prefix-identifiable gauge, explicit whole-cube rotation transport, and first-block preservation determine observable route classes independently of null scores. In development data, 114 of 130 eligible routes met the canonical first-block-preserving definition. Applying the frozen operator to 120 development-unseen reconstructions yielded 117 eligible routes, 116 with unique prefix gauge, and 105 canonical routes (89.7%), with zero local-reference fallback under a frozen 114-attempt reference bank. These results support the methodological claim that rich action traces are not self-interpreting: representation invariance, null behavior, multiplicity, transport, provenance, and claim authority require separate validation. They do not establish cognitive recovery or error prevalence. Genuinely post-freeze Roux validation remains pending because no qualifying fresh routes were available at the frozen authority check.
