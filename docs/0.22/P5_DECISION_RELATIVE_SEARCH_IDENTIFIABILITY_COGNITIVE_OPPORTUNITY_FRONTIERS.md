## 1. Directionality assessment: what is defensible and what was too ideal?

**Research-worthy direction:** Take actual finite physical control and TCS reachability seriously, while treating written FMC behavior as source-limited empirical process evidence. Compare **conditional laws and identification failures**, not numeric human deficits obtained by subtracting unrelated set sizes.

**Three methodological failure modes to block:**
1. **Ideal-oracle-to-human transfer:** A single tracked edge with a perfect intrinsic orientation bit is not human vision, full cube state representation or EO/DR/HTR/NISS search. Its Bellman costs are not subjective opportunity costs. An original 18-move face-turn menu and a reported 70-item EO *candidate pool* are different object types, so their cardinalities cannot be subtracted.
2. **Consideration/feasibility confusion:** A legal first turn can fail to admit a complete identifying policy under a seven-turn/four-read horizon, yet a person or software may still generate and discard it. Equating mathematically completable actions with psychologically considered actions is unjustified.
3. **Source/report-vs-latent-action confusion:** A retrospective reported candidate, a contemporaneous written move sequence, an actually evaluated branch, an unreported mental alternative and the submitted final move word have different evidence grades. The P4 source graph allows 41/40/36 node ontologies, not a full actual human timeline.

**Principled replacement:** Freeze a candidate grammar, action/observation model, resource context and selection mechanism before declaring a cognitive search barrier. Then seek contrasts constant across all source-compatible histories, or explicitly report sharp bounds and unknowns.

## 3. Choice correspondence theorem: sharp under its explicit assumptions

Let A be a frozen finite set of candidate first moves with known conditional optimal-continuation score c(a); define a latent **consideration menu** C as any nonempty subset of A. Under a **hypothetical weak-optimization** assumption that observed selected action a belongs to C and minimizes c on C, allowing arbitrary ties, a latent menu is consistent **iff** it contains a and excludes every b with c(b)<c(a). Each equal-or-worse b can be included or excluded independently. Therefore

Number of compatible menus = 2^(number of other equal-or-worse options).

This sharp set-theoretic enumeration is conditional on perfect knowledge of model c, exogenously fixed A, and cost-minimizing selection from C; it is NOT an observed human consideration count or newly invented general economic theory.

**Actual Rubik instance, same hypothetical observed root choice F:**
- If A contains only the **four budget-completable** first turns, every turn ties; **2^3 = 8** latent menus match F, and the considered-menu cardinality may be anywhere from 1 to 4.
- If A contains all **18 physically legal** first face turns and the 14 incompletable moves have infinite identification cost, **2^17 = 131072** latent menus match F, and considered-menu size could be anywhere from 1 to 18. This broader universe permits the agent to have considered then rejected a provably insufficient first turn.
- Identical **observed first move, same true physical state evolution and same normative optimized outcome** are compatible with radically different latent considered alternatives. Any stronger mental-process inference needs a separate credible observation of the generated/evaluated menus or a warranted attention model.

The switch from 8 to 131072 comes **solely from changing what is counted as an opportunity**, exactly the kind of hidden multivariate dependence the user identified earlier in M-star(L). Do not interpret the factor as a psychological effect, improvement, or human inefficiency. In particular an actual solver may not *know* a theoretically impossible move has infinite cost, so the strong model is an analyst counterfactual, not a literal account of human intentions.

**Conditional counterfactual regret:** For a fully specified finite mathematical controller, selecting a first move with score c(a) rather than global optimum incurs Delta(a) = c(a)−min c(A). This is calculable on tagged-edge belief states and positive on some physically reachable branches, but cannot be carried over as “human regret” or “wasted cognitive time” without an externally supported mapping between the two optimization tasks and their subjective costs.
