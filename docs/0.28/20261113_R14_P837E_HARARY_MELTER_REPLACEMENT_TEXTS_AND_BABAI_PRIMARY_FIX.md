## Three distinct, available replacement layers

1. **Khuller, Raghavachari & Rosenfeld (1996), *Landmarks in Graphs***, *Discrete Applied Mathematics* 70, 217–229, DOI [10.1016/0166-218X(95)00106-2](https://doi.org/10.1016/0166-218X(95)00106-2), [held Research OS PDF](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1tPF-UoAqrTXL4oYhm8Y9WyVanO7fY_7d/view). Their introduction defines unique distance signatures and reports Harary–Melter's characterization of the metric dimension of trees; **critically, they explicitly say Harary–Melter's *proof of Lemma 1 has an error*, then provide a similar characterization with their own proof** (1996 article pp. 218–220). Hence blindly inheriting the 1976 proof as authoritative would be methodologically unsafe.
2. **Bailey & Cameron (2011), *Base Size, Metric Dimension and Other Invariants of Groups and Graphs***, *Bulletin of the London Mathematical Society* 43, 209–242, DOI [10.1112/blms/bdq096](https://doi.org/10.1112/blms/bdq096), [held Research OS full journal PDF](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1PSS1zaeX_CD6VVuxrP1H6AfrYXscktuV/view). This is the most useful bridge for CUBE-REV: Definition 2.1 formalizes a permutation-group base as a subset with **trivial pointwise stabilizer**, whereas metric dimension identifies graph vertices by distinct **distance vectors**, explicitly **different observation channels**. The introduction states the metric dimension is an upper bound on the base size of the graph automorphism group. They directly track simultaneous rediscovery of related parameters. The bibliography lists Harary & Melter (1976) with **a second reference '4 (1977) 318'**, possibly an additional correction; **do not classify this 1977 entry as an erratum before an original issue-specific source is checked**.
3. **Tillquist, Frongillo & Lladser (2019 preprint), *Metric Dimension***, arXiv [1910.04103v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/1910.04103v1), [held seven-page 2019 version](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1ZHC9tfBU4pVrT0pRvM2Mo54ilWKJNXZz/view). It explicitly defines resolving sets as separating all vertex pairs by shortest-path distances. A **later 2023 SIAM Review journal survey**, *Getting the Lay of the Land in Discrete Space: A Survey of Metric Dimension and Its Applications*, DOI [10.1137/21M1409512](https://doi.org/10.1137/21M1409512), may supersede that short preprint; **do not treat the 2019 v1 PDF as byte-identical to the 2023 full survey** without acquiring the latter.

## Rigorous conceptual bridge (derived here; not quoted verbatim from Harary–Melter)

Given any state set X and labeled observation family \(f_s:X\to Y_s\), a selected sensor set S identifies X iff every distinct state pair \(x\ne y\) has at least one \(s\in S\) with \(f_s(x)\ne f_s(y)\). This is an abstract *separating family / hitting-set* condition. Ordinary graph metric dimension is its specialized instance \(X=V(\Gamma)\), \(f_s(x)=d_\Gamma(x,s)\), with allowable sensors graph vertices. CUBE-REV original-tag pose observation is instead \(X=B_r\subseteq G\), \(f_s(x)=\pi_s(x)\), i.e., **not a graph-distance response**. The observation-channel equality is neither assumed nor claimed. Hence Khuller et al. and Bailey–Cameron supply conceptual and historical prior art but **do not imply b6=16, b7=18 or the 906-layout collapse** without the exact P48 finite physical witnesses.
