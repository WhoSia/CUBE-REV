## Impact on CUBE-REV 0.29 AT mathematics

**Babai 1980 (new exact journal primary):** §2 defines graph canonical labeling using an ordering of vertices and the **neighborhood traces on a distinguishing set**. The algorithm enumerates candidate distinguishing ordered sets and rejects incomplete ones; §3 establishes the existence of small distinguishing sets for strongly regular graphs via a probabilistic bound. The observation channel is **static graph adjacency traces**, whereas CUBE-REV's is a sequence of **adaptive paired physical color replies**. This provides a historically prior *indistinguishability partition and canonicalization* concept, but neither the 18 lower bound nor the 19 upper bound follows from Babai's graph theorem. The paper's worst-case complexity motivation is relevant to distinguishing a fast heuristic explorer from a succinct proof checker.

**Tillquist–Frongillo–Lladser 2023 (now actual journal original):** The published version explicitly defines the metric dimension via injective shortest-path distance vectors (§2), studies computational hardness, and distinguishes several observer models, canonical labeling, abstract algebra and resolving sets. This clarifies a non-interchangeability issue: CUBE-REV's sensor output is categorical two-sticker colors and its objective uses **adaptively chosen physical frames**, not graph-distance landmark vectors. Cite the **2023 journal pagination 919–962** after this acquisition, keeping the 2021 29-page manuscript as a separate edition. The introduction cites Slater 1975 and Harary–Melter 1976, but their **original primary PDFs are still absent**, so secondary bibliography does not close those gaps.

**Azema–Couvissier–Richard 1972:** its sequential-system fault detection topic is adjacent to distinguishing experiments and potential adversarial response logic. It is not bibliographically Hennie 1964; no transfer of the 1964 experimental theory or original attribution is claimed. No mathematical bound from this control-theory paper is used in the Rubik proof.

**Brookes 2004 / Jones PRG-25 / Baldwin–Clark 2000:** these support proof-system modularity, state ownership, interference-safe observation boundaries, and decomposition of independently auditable certificate modules. They are **methodological analogies** not new proofs of Rubik state identifiability. In particular the 2021 vs 2023 article source identity, scanned monograph versus published journal, and proof-vs-receipt distinction are preserved.
