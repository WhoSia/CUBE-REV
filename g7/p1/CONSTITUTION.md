## Exact interpretation of the manipulation

The manipulation is not “easy versus hard” and not a guaranteed shorter total solution.

For the FLEXIBLE member, one extra phase-1 move can expose a phase-2 solved endpoint, so a longer phase-1 route may remain tied with the shortest total two-phase cost.

For the RIGID member, the +1 phase-1 routes in the certified frontier begin at positive phase-2 cost, so the extra phase-1 move does not create the same cost-neutral handoff.

G7-P1 therefore targets **lookahead-sensitive optimal-route flexibility**.

## Prospective hypotheses

### H1 — behavioral slack sensitivity
After matching current q, dG, d1 and shortest-entry continuation geometry, FLEXIBLE and RIGID states produce different first-action distributions and/or initial deliberation latency.

### H2 — expertise moderation
Any FLEXIBLE↔RIGID difference interacts with measured Cube expertise or algorithm familiarity rather than being assumed universal.

### H3 — policy-specific signal
If exact one-step-slack optimal first-action sets differ, human choices align with those sets above chance/individual baseline more strongly in the corresponding state family.

### H4 — null-compatible outcome
No behavioral difference is a valid result. It would reject the claim that this exact machine planning distinction is behaviorally salient under the chosen presentation/task.
