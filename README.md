CUBE-REV studies exact query complexity for finite hidden states under restricted adaptive observations, with the fixed-center Rubik's Cube as its first fully specified physical instance. The project combines combinatorial reductions, certificate-producing search, executable policies, and Lean verification.

### Signed matching rigidity: an explanatory structural gate

For a feasible bipartite compatibility matrix with reference perfect matching M, contract the matched edges and direct an arc i -> j whenever row i can take M(j), omitting loops. Every other perfect matching corresponds exactly to a vertex-disjoint collection of simple directed cycles; a cycle of length l changes relative permutation sign by (-1)^(l-1). Thus the sign class of M has exactly one matching iff there is no odd directed cycle and no pair of vertex-disjoint even directed cycles. Both sign classes have at most one matching iff there is no directed cycle, or exactly one simple directed cycle and its length is even. Equivalently, there is at most one non-singleton strongly connected component, which must be a directed even cycle. Given one matching, the latter test is linear in graph size with standard SCC algorithms (the audit uses transitive closure).

For cube residual edges, if each observed cubie has at least one color and exactly one residual slot is entirely unread, each matching has one orientation completion after imposing the global flip constraint. Therefore this test characterizes the sign-indexed terminal leaf condition in that submodel. Two or more unread edge slots instead admit orientation ambiguity even for fixed matching and sign. Zero unread slots need explicit flip filtering; do not apply the raw matching criterion as an equivalence in that case.

The registered audit checks all 4,165 loopless digraphs on 1..4 labeled vertices with diagonal matching and all 106,920 four-edge residual matrices (choose 4 of the 12 edges of K(2,2,2), three ordered singleton colors, one blank row): 45,126 feasible, 34,344 sign-injective, direct enumeration/cycle/SCC discrepancies zero. These are matrix counts, not frequencies of frozen policy leaves or uniform physical states. No new 19-frame proof is claimed. Classical matching/cycle theory; compare Robertson, Seymour & Thomas (1999), *Permanents, Pfaffian orientations, and even directed circuits*, Annals 150:929-975, https://annals.math.princeton.edu/1999/150-3/p05 (not a claim of identical theorem).

### Four-character counting and the actual frozen leaves

Let A0,A1 be the compatibility matrices for edge orientation 0 and 1, with rows in original position order and columns in increasing label rank. The number N(p,f) of completions with inversion parity p and flip residue f equals

    [per(A0+A1) + (-1)^f per(A0-A1)
     + (-1)^p det(A0+A1) + (-1)^(p+f) det(A0-A1)] / 4.

This follows by expanding products over assignments and applying character orthogonality on C2 x C2. The implemented counter uses the equivalent four-sector subset DP, not direct determinant evaluation. For an already used label set U, appending label j contributes inversion parity |{i in U : i>j}| mod 2 and the selected orientation bit. No frozen search engine is imported.

The audit reads the compressed frozen upper-policy artifact, checks both pinned SHA-256 values, traverses all 52 representative trees, and counts completions from each complete leaf observation record. Observed 141097 nodes, 73962 leaves, 144736 flip-zero completions; 70774 two-candidate leaves (opposite signs), 3188 singleton leaves. Last edge read counts 0/1/2: 17048/54690/2224. In 13535 leaves with no blank slot, ignoring flip parity gives multiple matchings in at least one permutation-sign sector. These are counterfactual counts on the same records, not a verification of the altered game's policy or uniform physical probabilities. The four-sector leaf check and mass conservation are complementary to the existing exhaustive response verifier, not its replacement. Tested also 23760 synthetic three-edge records by explicit enumeration. The prior SCC result directly applies to the 17048 one-blank leaves, not all leaves.

### Prefix polytopes and forest enumeration

For a graph H of distinct two-color edge types, let r(S) count edges touching color subset S. This coverage function is integer, monotone and submodular. By Hall, a color histogram x from k distinct edges is feasible precisely when x is a nonnegative integer vector, sum x=k and x(S)<=r(S) for all S. For k<|E| in the cube model at least one blank edge allows the flip residue to be corrected. For k=|E| the support below is the orientation-unrestricted combinatorial histogram support; fixed flip constraints may remove some histograms.

Equivalently the unrestricted prefix layers are lattice points of the Minkowski sum of coordinate triangles conv(0,e_u,e_v), sliced at sum x=k. Hall ensures the integer-point equivalence: assign each color token to a distinct incident edge; conversely all points in the Minkowski sum obey the cuts. In the top layer this is the graphical zonotope sum [e_u,e_v]. The classical forest formula (Postnikov, *Permutohedra, Associahedra, and Beyond*, Proposition 2.4, https://math.mit.edu/~apost/papers/permutohedron_full.pdf) gives 1083 top-layer points for K(2,2,2). Direct forest coefficients by edge count are 1,12,66,212,408,384, giving Ehrhart polynomial 1+12t+66t^2+212t^3+408t^4+384t^5. All 13 unrestricted layers: 1,6,21,56,126,246,426,666,939,1198,1362,1362,1083; 7492 total. The audit compares all 15625 bounded lattice candidates with endpoint-selection DP, all 4096 submodular inequalities, and forest enumeration. This extends the previous 11-color histogram view to the entire prefix polytope; it is not a new 19-frame optimality proof or a novelty claim.

### Parity-constrained discrete polymatroids: rank blindness and exchange failure

For each full color histogram x, orient each edge toward its selected endpoint. Two orientations of the same histogram differ by a balanced directed subgraph of the first orientation, decomposable into edge-disjoint directed cycles. Consequently a histogram admits both orientation parities iff any (equivalently every) orientation in its fiber contains an odd directed cycle. A cycle reversal changes orientation parity by its length modulo 2. This is a parity refinement of classical indegree-sequence/cycle-reversal theory (Gioan 2007, Proposition 4.10), not a claim of a new general cycle-reversal theorem.

In the fixed EighteenReadData label convention, the K(2,2,2) top layer has 222 even-only, 216 odd-only and 645 shared histograms, hence legal flip-zero support 867 and flip-one support 861. Both parity-restricted families violate the necessary base-exchange axiom of discrete polymatroids (Herzog & Hibi 2002, Theorem 2.3). In URFDLB order, even-sector x=(4,0,2,4,0,2), y=(4,0,2,3,2,1) and i=B force the sole possible exchange j=L; x-e_B+e_L=(4,0,2,4,1,1) has counts (0,1), so is not even-feasible. The odd-sector witness is in the audit. All 2,741,706 unrestricted ordered-pair/excess-coordinate tests pass; even and odd failures are 37,596 and 40,968 respectively. Counts are of exchange obligations, not distinct pairs.

For a nonempty graph with a fixed reference orientation and weight vector w, unrestricted support maximum H(w)=sum_uv max(w_u,w_v). Choose a maximizing orientation of parity b, and let delta=min_uv |w_u-w_v|. The parity-p maximum is H(w) if p=b, otherwise H(w)-delta: flipping a minimum-gap edge suffices, and any opposite-parity assignment must flip an odd, nonempty set of edges. This holds also with ties. For indicator weights of subsets S, delta=0 iff some edge does not cross the cut. Thus on any nonbipartite graph both parity supports have identical subset-rank maxima, while a bipartite graph with at least one edge has a cut where one parity maximum drops. For the cube all 64 ranks coincide although the weighted objective w=(5,0,3,4,2,1) separates the supports (even maximum 44, odd 45). Subset-rank descriptions alone therefore cannot recover either parity family.

The graph zonotope vertices are exactly acyclic-orientation histograms: strict compatible topological weights expose each acyclic orientation uniquely; a generic exposing weight for a vertex chooses an acyclic orientation. Enumeration gives 210 even and 216 odd vertices. Every odd-only histogram is an odd vertex; the even-only set contains 210 vertices and 12 nonvertices. All 12 latter points are midpoints of pairs of odd-support histograms. Thus the legal even support is precisely all integral points of its own convex hull, whereas the odd hull has exactly 12 missing lattice points. This conclusion is for the fixed edge-orientation convention, and the odd sector is a counterfactual control rather than a legal complete cube edge state. Audits independently enumerate all orientations, exchange obligations, integer midpoints, and 729 weight vectors in {-1,0,1}^6. The parity support-function proof is elementary; the cube-specific enumerations are computational. These are optional 0.30 structural investigations, not dependencies of the accepted 0.29 19-frame theorem.

### Exact exchange criterion, parity automata, and a classification for simple graphs

Fix an orientation D realizing a histogram x in parity sector p. To transfer one selected endpoint count from i to j without changing parity, reverse an edge set with boundary e_j-e_i and even cardinality. Such a set decomposes into a directed simple path from j to i and edge-disjoint directed cycles in D (orientations point toward selected endpoints). Hence x-e_i+e_j is feasible in the same sector iff there is either an even-length directed simple j-to-i path, or an odd-length directed simple j-to-i path and an edge-disjoint odd directed simple cycle. Necessity follows by removing a path from the difference of two orientations, then decomposing the remaining balanced directed subgraph into cycles; sufficiency follows by reversing the displayed path or path-plus-cycle. The correcting cycle need not be connected to the path. The ordinary parity-lift automaton (v,b) -> (u,b xor 1) recognizes even walks, not edge sets: it may reuse edges and cannot account for disconnected correcting cycles. The audit tests all 4096 orientations of K(2,2,2), and all 108384 admissible endpoint transfer requests (including repeated histograms represented by different orientations). Exact criterion and direct sector membership agree in every case; 93156 feasible requests. The parity-lift reachability test has 6816 false positives and 864 false negatives; even simple paths alone miss 15612 feasible requests. These counts describe the fixed orientation model, not state-identification policy costs.

**Classification (mathematical proof, prior-art novelty open).** For any finite nonempty simple undirected graph H, the number of its two orientation-parity histogram supports that are discrete-polymatroid base sets is exactly: two if |E|=1; one if |E|=2 and the edges meet; zero otherwise. An empty graph has only one nonempty parity support, a singleton. Reference reversal only permutes the parity names. Proof:
- One edge is immediate. Two adjacent edges give, after subtracting fixed zero coordinates, either the two histograms both selected at the shared vertex / both at the distinct leaves (exchange fails), or the two histograms with one selected at the shared vertex (exchange succeeds).
- If H has two disjoint edges, assign equal weights within the endpoint pair of each, with distinct weights between the two pairs and all other individual vertices. All other edges have a strict maximizing endpoint. In either parity the exposed face consists of two opposite corners of the two-edge rectangle. Choose x,y from those corners and an excess coordinate i of x in the lower-weight pair. Its only deficit candidates are in the same pair (the exchange flips one tied edge and changes parity) or in the higher-weight pair (the exchange exceeds the maximum). Both fail. This proves both sectors fail, even with additional edges.
- A simple graph with at least two edges and no two disjoint edges is a star or the triangle, ignoring isolated vertices: take two edges uv,uw; an edge missing u must be vw, and then no further edge is allowed. For a star with >=3 edges, tie the center with two leaves, give the other leaves distinct unequal weights, and choose the side of one other leaf's weight to make the fixed choices have whichever parity is required. The two optimizing configurations in that sector have both tied edges selecting the center or both selecting the leaves. A one-unit exchange from the center to either leaf would toggle exactly one tied edge while remaining optimal, impossible in the same parity.
- For the triangle (indeed any nonbipartite simple graph) both sectors have the full unrestricted subset-rank maxima by the preceding support-function theorem. Neither support is full: take a strict vertex order with endpoints of an edge adjacent, then swap these two endpoints to expose unique maximizing orientations of opposite parity. A discrete-polymatroid base set is precisely the integral points of its base polytope, determined by its subset rank function (Herzog-Hibi, Discrete Polymatroids, Thms 2.3, 2.7). Thus neither sector can be a discrete-polymatroid base set. This argument proves a stronger statement for all nonbipartite simple graphs, while the disjoint-edge and star arguments also cover bipartite graphs.

Direct finite audit enumerated all 1094 nonempty labeled simple graphs with 2..5 vertices, both sectors (2188), and found no discrepancy with the classification. This is implementation evidence alongside the general proof, not a replacement for it. The theorem concerns histogram projection of orientation parity, not arbitrary matroid parity, parity-constrained optimization on matroids, or the 19-frame theorem.

### Bounded symmetric exchange via zero-sum decomposition

A constant-sum jump system has the ordinary unit base-exchange property: in its two-step axiom the first unit step cannot stay in the fixed-sum set, and the second must cancel the sum change. Therefore the previously proved non-polymatroid sectors are not jump systems either (Bouchet & Cunningham 1995, Murota 2006). In contrast the parity class of subsets of edge indices is an even delta-matroid under symmetric difference. Its signed incidence-map image is not ordinary coordinate aggregation; this explains why closure theorems for aggregation of jump systems do not apply.

**General bounded symmetric exchange theorem.** Let I be a finite family of independent local-choice indices. Each choice a at index i has a color v(i,a) and a charge g(i,a) in a finite abelian group G. For a fixed total charge q, let B_q be the set of color histograms of all choice assignments with sum of charges q. For x,y in B_q and a surplus coordinate u (x_u>y_u), there exists a nonzero transfer vector d, coordinatewise directed from x toward y and using at least one unit of the surplus at u, such that x+d and y-d both belong to B_q, both lie coordinatewise between x and y, sum(d)=0, and ||d||_1 <= 2D(G). Here D(G) is the classical Davenport constant, maximum length of a minimal zero-sum sequence (Gao & Geroldinger 2006, Definition 2.1, https://doi.org/10.1016/j.exmath.2006.07.002).

Proof: choose lifts of x and y with the same total charge. For each index at which the two choices differ, create a directed arc from the color in the first lift to the color in the second (allow loops and parallel arcs, distinguish by index), labelled by the charge increment. The difference multigraph decomposes into edge-disjoint directed simple paths from surplus to deficit coordinates and directed cycles, including loops. Each path contributes one unit transfer, each cycle zero histogram change. The sum of the group charges of all components is zero. Partition the component charge sequence into minimal nonempty zero-sum blocks, each of size at most D(G). Choose the block containing one path starting at u. Swap the local choices at the indices of that block between the two lifts. Because the block has zero charge and uses distinct indices, both modified lifts have the required charge. The resulting d is a sum of at most D(G) surplus-to-deficit transfers with no cancellation, so the required bounds follow. Repeating such exchanges reduces L1 distance. Sharpness over the general model follows by taking D(G) independent binary choices with disjoint color pairs and charge increments forming a longest minimal zero-sum sequence: only all-or-none flips maintain the charge. In the trivial group D(G)=1; D(C_m)=m by prefix sums and the repeated-generator lower witness.

**Separable discrete convex objective corollary.** For F(x)=sum_v phi_v(x_v) with nondecreasing discrete differences on the relevant integer intervals, a point x in B_q is globally minimizing iff no feasible point within L1 distance 2D(G) has a strictly smaller F. Given any y, the above decomposition partitions all changed indices into zero-charge blocks giving feasible x+d_j, with d_j coordinatewise aligned with y-x and sum_j d_j=y-x. Convexity of the univariate increments in either monotone direction implies F(y)-F(x) >= sum_j [F(x+d_j)-F(x)]. Therefore any better y requires an improving block. This is a local-global statement, not a polynomial-time algorithm for finding a neighbor or checking membership. Compare Murota's local optimality results on jump systems and component-sum constraints; these histogram sectors need not themselves be jump systems. Novelty relative to discrete convexity, congruence-constrained optimization and Graver-basis methods remains open.

**Physical-coordinate test.** The exact code uses EighteenReadData edge labels and FaceTurns.cornerNames = UFR,URB,ULF,UBL,DRF,DBR,DFL,DLB with cyclic shift j as the charge. For one selected sticker per distinct cubie, all 4096 edge choice assignments and 6561 corner choice assignments were counted. Edge sectors 0/1 have 867/861 histograms. Corner unrestricted support 795, residues 0/1/2 have 609/660/660 histograms. Test every ordered pair and surplus coordinate for the minimum number of simultaneous monotone unit transfers that preserve BOTH endpoints' residue feasibility. Edge legal residue zero: 1,631,310 obligations require one, 105,468 two (1,736,778 total). Odd: 1,597,338 one, 116,448 two. Corner legal residue zero: 767,352 one, 62,292 two, 2,760 three (832,404 total); each nonzero residue 945,084 one, 34,200 two, 384 three. The bounds 2 and 3 are attained in these actual histogram supports. In URFDLB order a corner witness is x=(4,0,0,4,0,0), y=(4,0,1,1,1,1), excess D; no symmetric monotone exchange using one or two transfers works. These are exact histograms in the local orientation-choice model with the physical twist convention, not a claim about full physical-state query complexity or permutation coupling. No change to the 0.29 theorem.

### Target-relative observation complexity: common permutation sign

The physical group is the fiber product of the orientation-sum-zero corner and edge wreath-product factors over permutation sign (Mereb & Vendramin, *Rubik's as Galois': The inverse Galois problem for the Rubik's Cube group*, §2.2, arXiv:2411.11566v3). We use the standard sufficiency of the three legality conditions; no new Lean proof of that sufficiency is claimed here. Consequently, compatible edge and corner posteriors combine freely subject to equality of their signs.

**Certificate minima (state-dependent addresses).** For either factor, the minimum number of single-sticker observations of a specified arrangement that forces its permutation sign is independent of the arrangement. Reindex the positions by actual cubie labels and cyclically shift the local coordinates by the actual twists; the sum of those shifts is zero, and reindexing only changes sign by a constant. This bijection maps the actual state to the solved reference, preserves the allowed sticker subsets and transports opposite-sign witnesses. Two distinct local sticker colors in their known cyclic order identify an oriented corner (checked for either relative offset); two edge stickers identify an oriented edge. Hence three corner reads on the same cubie are redundant. A certificate cannot leave two blank pieces (swap), two single-read pieces reporting the same color (exchange the labels and carry orientation with the necessary local-side offset; the offsets cancel), or a single-read piece whose reported color occurs on the sole blank piece (swap and choose the blank orientation to preserve the sum). The remaining patterns have at most one blank and distinct singleton colors, disjoint from the blank's colors. Enumerate these reduced patterns on the solved reference, and for each enumerate residual label assignments and compatible orientations, searching for an opposite-sign, residue-zero completion. All rejected patterns have explicit swap obstructions above. Results: 14891 edge patterns, 14109 sign certificates, minimum 18; 5873 corner patterns, 4389 certificates, minimum 11; all 464 cost-10 corner patterns fail. Witnesses and full counts in the registered audit. These are certificate sizes, NOT minimax query depths.

**Fixed sign depth.** For a factor of n pieces, 2(n-1) fixed observations suffice by reading two stickers on n-1 pieces, leaving the last identity forced. Fewer than 2(n-1) reads leave either two blank pieces, a blank and a singleton, or two singletons. Since the unknown arrangement is unrestricted subject to its orientation sum, place two labels sharing a color in those positions, arrange the relevant singleton readings to show that shared color and use another position to correct the total orientation residue if needed. The swap obstructions give two compatible arrangements of opposite signs. This proves fixed edge depth 22, corner depth 14 (for the actual n=12,8 factors). Combined paired fixed sign depth is 14: below 14 both factor queries have ambiguous sign histories, and the fiber product combines these into two indistinguishable physical states.

**Exact adaptive corner-sign recursion.** State = sorted multiset R of singly observed colors, remaining label mask L (one label per unresolved position), residual twist sum r modulo 3. There are |L|-|R| blank positions. A singly observed position is normalized so its first observed sticker is local side 0. Full observations identify label and normalized twist, then remove that row and label. Sorting/reindexing changes only the known permutation-sign offset, which is irrelevant to whether both signs occur. The controller retains the actual row/label mapping for recovering the absolute sign. In a state, the two-bit sign support is computed by recursively assigning one label containing the first color to that row; its required twist is the cyclic index of the color, and the inversion contribution is the number of unused smaller labels. With only blank rows left the support is both signs if at least two labels remain, singleton if one remains, and at zero labels feasible only when r=0. Blank twists can realize every residual residue. A state with singleton sign support is terminal. Otherwise every informative action is (i) read a blank row on any local side j=0,1,2, append the reported color and update r to r+j mod 3; (ii) read either of the other two sides of a singleton row, each of which identifies its label and twist, remove the label and subtract the normalized twist. Enumerate only feasible replies using the sign-support recurrence. Repeated reads and third reads add no information. The Bellman value is 1+min_action max_feasible_reply child value. The remaining read capacity decreases at every transition, so exact recursion is finite; quotienting identical rows and retaining r preserves all actions up to a known permutation-sign gauge. With alpha pruning of actions whose worst observed branch already meets the incumbent, audit returns 13 for the root and for each of the three first-side choices, 107472 cached ambiguous depth states, 218370 support states. Separately, seven fixed first reads give 642 realizable sorted prefixes and restricted continuation depths 4:48, 5:132, 6:462. Thus a simple seven-first policy also attains 13; full minimax excludes 12. This is a computer-assisted exact result for the stated recurrence, not kernel-verified.

**Physical paired sign transfer.** Upper bound: run the corner policy and ignore edge replies. Lower bound: suppose a paired policy of at most 12 reads identifies the common sign. Simulate it on one fixed residue-zero edge arrangement while allowing all residue-zero corner arrangements; every prefix of the fixed edge replies has both sign possibilities by the 18-read per-state edge certificate lower bound. For every corner trajectory, each compatible corner sign can therefore be completed to a legal paired state with the same transcript, by the fiber-product property. Thus the simulated policy must itself identify the corner sign within 12, contradicting the corner minimax result. Joint address realizability and free legal turns are the existing physical-model assumptions. Hence the paired common-sign optimum is 13, compared with fixed 14 and full reconstruction 19. This does not prove an improved full reconstruction policy or a new 0.29 axiom dependency. Independent recurrence audit and formalization are open.

### Closest structural comparators

Nägele, Santiago & Zenklusen, *Congruency-Constrained TU Problems Beyond the Bimodular Case*, arXiv:2109.03148v3, Definition 6 and Lemmas 7–8, https://arxiv.org/pdf/2109.03148, and Nägele et al., *Advances on strictly Delta-modular IPs*, Math. Program. 210 (2025), https://doi.org/10.1007/s10107-024-02148-2, Lemmas 11, 19 and 35, already use elementary TU decompositions with congruence/group constraints and zero-sum removal for proximity/feasibility. The earlier local-choice model admits a bipartite incidence lift: choice variables z_(i,a) binary, sum_a z_(i,a)=1, histogram x_v=sum_(i,a):v(i,a)=v z_(i,a). Its incidence matrix is TU after row sign changes. The group equation fixes total charge. Thus general decomposition and group-charge grouping are closely anticipated; keep the specialized sharp projected symmetric-exchange bound as a reconstruction with prior-art priority unresolved, not an independent new field. The separable convex local-global argument is the usual conformal augmentation inequality, familiar from Graver-basis optimization; the possible residual is the explicit D(G) radius in the projected histogram coordinates. Prioritize the nonconstant physical target complexity over further generic exchange theory.
