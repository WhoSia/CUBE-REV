### Signed matching rigidity: an explanatory structural gate

For a feasible bipartite compatibility matrix with reference perfect matching M, contract the matched edges and direct an arc i -> j whenever row i can take M(j), omitting loops. Every other perfect matching corresponds exactly to a vertex-disjoint collection of simple directed cycles; a cycle of length l changes relative permutation sign by (-1)^(l-1). Thus the sign class of M has exactly one matching iff there is no odd directed cycle and no pair of vertex-disjoint even directed cycles. Both sign classes have at most one matching iff there is no directed cycle, or exactly one simple directed cycle and its length is even. Equivalently, there is at most one non-singleton strongly connected component, which must be a directed even cycle. Given one matching, the latter test is linear in graph size with standard SCC algorithms (the audit uses transitive closure).

For cube residual edges, if each observed cubie has at least one color and exactly one residual slot is entirely unread, each matching has one orientation completion after imposing the global flip constraint. Therefore this test characterizes the sign-indexed terminal leaf condition in that submodel. Two or more unread edge slots instead admit orientation ambiguity even for fixed matching and sign. Zero unread slots need explicit flip filtering; do not apply the raw matching criterion as an equivalence in that case.

The registered audit checks all 4,165 loopless digraphs on 1..4 labeled vertices with diagonal matching and all 106,920 four-edge residual matrices (choose 4 of the 12 edges of K(2,2,2), three ordered singleton colors, one blank row): 45,126 feasible, 34,344 sign-injective, direct enumeration/cycle/SCC discrepancies zero. These are matrix counts, not frequencies of frozen policy leaves or uniform physical states. No new 19-frame proof is claimed. Classical matching/cycle theory; compare Robertson, Seymour & Thomas (1999), *Permanents, Pfaffian orientations, and even directed circuits*, Annals 150:929-975, https://annals.math.princeton.edu/1999/150-3/p05 (not a claim of identical theorem).

### Four-character counting and the actual frozen leaves

Let A0,A1 be the compatibility matrices for edge orientation 0 and 1, with rows in original position order and columns in increasing label rank. The number N(p,f) of completions with inversion parity p and flip residue f equals

    [per(A0+A1) + (-1)^f per(A0-A1)
     + (-1)^p det(A0+A1) + (-1)^(p+f) det(A0-A1)] / 4.

This follows by expanding products over assignments and applying character orthogonality on C2 x C2. The implemented counter uses the equivalent four-sector subset DP, not direct determinant evaluation. For an already used label set U, appending label j contributes inversion parity |{i in U : i>j}| mod 2 and the selected orientation bit. No frozen search engine is imported.

### Prefix polytopes and forest enumeration

For a graph H of distinct two-color edge types, let r(S) count edges touching color subset S. This coverage function is integer, monotone and submodular. By Hall, a color histogram x from k distinct edges is feasible precisely when x is a nonnegative integer vector, sum x=k and x(S)<=r(S) for all S. For k<|E| in the cube model at least one blank edge allows the flip residue to be corrected. For k=|E| the support below is the orientation-unrestricted combinatorial histogram support; fixed flip constraints may remove some histograms.

Equivalently the unrestricted prefix layers are lattice points of the Minkowski sum of coordinate triangles conv(0,e_u,e_v), sliced at sum x=k. Hall ensures the integer-point equivalence: assign each color token to a distinct incident edge; conversely all points in the Minkowski sum obey the cuts. In the top layer this is the graphical zonotope sum [e_u,e_v]. The classical forest formula (Postnikov, *Permutohedra, Associahedra, and Beyond*, Proposition 2.4, https://math.mit.edu/~apost/papers/permutohedron_full.pdf) gives 1083 top-layer points for K(2,2,2). Direct forest coefficients by edge count are 1,12,66,212,408,384, giving Ehrhart polynomial 1+12t+66t^2+212t^3+408t^4+384t^5. All 13 unrestricted layers: 1,6,21,56,126,246,426,666,939,1198,1362,1362,1083; 7492 total. The audit compares all 15625 bounded lattice candidates with endpoint-selection DP, all 4096 submodular inequalities, and forest enumeration. This extends the previous 11-color histogram view to the entire prefix polytope; it is not a new 19-frame optimality proof or a novelty claim.

### Parity-constrained discrete polymatroids: rank blindness and exchange failure

For each full color histogram x, orient each edge toward its selected endpoint. Two orientations of the same histogram differ by a balanced directed subgraph of the first orientation, decomposable into edge-disjoint directed cycles. Consequently a histogram admits both orientation parities iff any (equivalently every) orientation in its fiber contains an odd directed cycle. A cycle reversal changes orientation parity by its length modulo 2. This is a parity refinement of classical indegree-sequence/cycle-reversal theory (Gioan 2007, Proposition 4.10), not a claim of a new general cycle-reversal theorem.

In the fixed EighteenReadData label convention, the K(2,2,2) top layer has 222 even-only, 216 odd-only and 645 shared histograms, hence legal flip-zero support 867 and flip-one support 861. Both parity-restricted families violate the necessary base-exchange axiom of discrete polymatroids (Herzog & Hibi 2002, Theorem 2.3). In URFDLB order, even-sector x=(4,0,2,4,0,2), y=(4,0,2,3,2,1) and i=B force the sole possible exchange j=L; x-e_B+e_L=(4,0,2,4,1,1) has counts (0,1), so is not even-feasible. The odd-sector witness is in the audit. All 2,741,706 unrestricted ordered-pair/excess-coordinate tests pass; even and odd failures are 37,596 and 40,968 respectively. Counts are of exchange obligations, not distinct pairs.

For a nonempty graph with a fixed reference orientation and weight vector w, unrestricted support maximum H(w)=sum_uv max(w_u,w_v). Choose a maximizing orientation of parity b, and let delta=min_uv |w_u-w_v|. The parity-p maximum is H(w) if p=b, otherwise H(w)-delta: flipping a minimum-gap edge suffices, and any opposite-parity assignment must flip an odd, nonempty set of edges. This holds also with ties. For indicator weights of subsets S, delta=0 iff some edge does not cross the cut. Thus on any nonbipartite graph both parity supports have identical subset-rank maxima, while a bipartite graph with at least one edge has a cut where one parity maximum drops. For the cube all 64 ranks coincide although the weighted objective w=(5,0,3,4,2,1) separates the supports (even maximum 44, odd 45). Subset-rank descriptions alone therefore cannot recover either parity family.

The graph zonotope vertices are exactly acyclic-orientation histograms: strict compatible topological weights expose each acyclic orientation uniquely; a generic exposing weight for a vertex chooses an acyclic orientation. Enumeration gives 210 even and 216 odd vertices. Every odd-only histogram is an odd vertex; the even-only set contains 210 vertices and 12 nonvertices. All 12 latter points are midpoints of pairs of odd-support histograms. Thus the legal even support is precisely all integral points of its own convex hull, whereas the odd hull has exactly 12 missing lattice points. This conclusion is for the fixed edge-orientation convention, and the odd sector is a counterfactual control rather than a legal complete cube edge state. Audits independently enumerate all orientations, exchange obligations, integer midpoints, and 729 weight vectors in {-1,0,1}^6. The parity support-function proof is elementary; the cube-specific enumerations are computational. These are optional 0.30 structural investigations, not dependencies of the accepted 0.29 19-frame theorem.

### Exact exchange criterion, parity automata, and a classification for simple graphs

Fix an orientation D realizing a histogram x in parity sector p. To transfer one selected endpoint count from i to j without changing parity, reverse an edge set with boundary e_j-e_i and even cardinality. Such a set decomposes into a directed simple path from j to i and edge-disjoint directed cycles in D (orientations point toward selected endpoints). Hence x-e_i+e_j is feasible in the same sector iff there is either an even-length directed simple j-to-i path, or an odd-length directed simple j-to-i path and an edge-disjoint odd directed simple cycle. Necessity follows by removing a path from the difference of two orientations, then decomposing the remaining balanced directed subgraph into cycles; sufficiency follows by reversing the displayed path or path-plus-cycle. The correcting cycle need not be connected to the path. The ordinary parity-lift automaton (v,b) -> (u,b xor 1) recognizes even walks, not edge sets: it may reuse edges and cannot account for disconnected correcting cycles. The audit tests all 4096 orientations of K(2,2,2), and all 108384 admissible endpoint transfer requests (including repeated histograms represented by different orientations). Exact criterion and direct sector membership agree in every case; 93156 feasible requests. The parity-lift reachability test has 6816 false positives and 864 false negatives; even simple paths alone miss 15612 feasible requests. These counts describe the fixed orientation model, not state-identification policy costs.

**Classification (mathematical proof, prior-art novelty open).** For any finite nonempty simple undirected graph H, the number of its two orientation-parity histogram supports that are discrete-polymatroid base sets is exactly: two if |E|=1; one if |E|=2 and the edges meet; zero otherwise. An empty graph has only one nonempty parity support, a singleton. Reference reversal only permutes the parity names. Proof:
- One edge is immediate. Two adjacent edges give, after subtracting fixed zero coordinates, either the two histograms both selected at the shared vertex / both at the distinct leaves (exchange fails), or the two histograms with one selected at the shared vertex (exchange succeeds).
- If H has two disjoint edges, assign equal weights within the endpoint pair of each, with distinct weights between the two pairs and all other individual vertices. All other edges have a strict maximizing endpoint. In either parity the exposed face consists of two opposite corners of the two-edge rectangle. Choose x,y from those corners and an excess coordinate i of x in the lower-weight pair. Its only deficit candidates are in the same pair (the exchange flips one tied edge and changes parity) or in the higher-weight pair (the exchange exceeds the maximum). Both fail. This proves both sectors fail, even with additional edges.
- A simple graph with at least two edges and no two disjoint edges is a star or the triangle, ignoring isolated vertices: take two edges uv,uw; an edge missing u must be vw, and then no further edge is allowed. For a star with >=3 edges, tie the center with two leaves, give the other leaves distinct unequal weights, and choose the side of one other leaf's weight to make the fixed choices have whichever parity is required. The two optimizing configurations in that sector have both tied edges selecting the center or both selecting the leaves. A one-unit exchange from the center to either leaf would toggle exactly one tied edge while remaining optimal, impossible in the same parity.
- For the triangle (indeed any nonbipartite simple graph) both sectors have the full unrestricted subset-rank maxima by the preceding support-function theorem. Neither support is full: take a strict vertex order with endpoints of an edge adjacent, then swap these two endpoints to expose unique maximizing orientations of opposite parity. A discrete-polymatroid base set is precisely the integral points of its base polytope, determined by its subset rank function (Herzog-Hibi, Discrete Polymatroids, Thms 2.3, 2.7). Thus neither sector can be a discrete-polymatroid base set. This argument proves a stronger statement for all nonbipartite simple graphs, while the disjoint-edge and star arguments also cover bipartite graphs.

Direct finite audit enumerated all 1094 nonempty labeled simple graphs with 2..5 vertices, both sectors (2188), and found no discrepancy with the classification. This is implementation evidence alongside the general proof, not a replacement for it. The theorem concerns histogram projection of orientation parity, not arbitrary matroid parity, parity-constrained optimization on matroids, or the 19-frame theorem.

### Bounded symmetric exchange via zero-sum decomposition

A constant-sum jump system has the ordinary unit base-exchange property: in its two-step axiom the first unit step cannot stay in the fixed-sum set, and the second must cancel the sum change. Therefore the previously proved non-polymatroid sectors are not jump systems either (Bouchet & Cunningham 1995, Murota 2006). In contrast the parity class of subsets of edge indices is an even delta-matroid under symmetric difference. Its signed incidence-map image is not ordinary coordinate aggregation; this explains why closure theorems for aggregation of jump systems do not apply.

**General bounded symmetric exchange theorem.** Let I be a finite family of independent local-choice indices. Each choice a at index i has a color v(i,a) and a charge g(i,a) in a finite abelian group G. For a fixed total charge q, let B_q be the set of color histograms of all choice assignments with sum of charges q. For x,y in B_q and a surplus coordinate u (x_u>y_u), there exists a nonzero transfer vector d, coordinatewise directed from x toward y and using at least one unit of the surplus at u, such that x+d and y-d both belong to B_q, both lie coordinatewise between x and y, sum(d)=0, and ||d||_1 <= 2D(G). Here D(G) is the classical Davenport constant, maximum length of a minimal zero-sum sequence (Gao & Geroldinger 2006, Definition 2.1, https://doi.org/10.1016/j.exmath.2006.07.002).

Proof: choose lifts of x and y with the same total charge. For each index at which the two choices differ, create a directed arc from the color in the first lift to the color in the second (allow loops and parallel arcs, distinguish by index), labelled by the charge increment. The difference multigraph decomposes into edge-disjoint directed simple paths from surplus to deficit coordinates and directed cycles, including loops. Each path contributes one unit transfer, each cycle zero histogram change. The sum of the group charges of all components is zero. Partition the component charge sequence into minimal nonempty zero-sum blocks, each of size at most D(G). Choose the block containing one path starting at u. Swap the local choices at the indices of that block between the two lifts. Because the block has zero charge and uses distinct indices, both modified lifts have the required charge. The resulting d is a sum of at most D(G) surplus-to-deficit transfers with no cancellation, so the required bounds follow. Repeating such exchanges reduces L1 distance. Sharpness over the general model follows by taking D(G) independent binary choices with disjoint color pairs and charge increments forming a longest minimal zero-sum sequence: only all-or-none flips maintain the charge. In the trivial group D(G)=1; D(C_m)=m by prefix sums and the repeated-generator lower witness.

**Separable discrete convex objective corollary.** For F(x)=sum_v phi_v(x_v) with nondecreasing discrete differences on the relevant integer intervals, a point x in B_q is globally minimizing iff no feasible point within L1 distance 2D(G) has a strictly smaller F. Given any y, the above decomposition partitions all changed indices into zero-charge blocks giving feasible x+d_j, with d_j coordinatewise aligned with y-x and sum_j d_j=y-x. Convexity of the univariate increments in either monotone direction implies F(y)-F(x) >= sum_j [F(x+d_j)-F(x)]. Therefore any better y requires an improving block. This is a local-global statement, not a polynomial-time algorithm for finding a neighbor or checking membership. Compare Murota's local optimality results on jump systems and component-sum constraints; these histogram sectors need not themselves be jump systems. Novelty relative to discrete convexity, congruence-constrained optimization and Graver-basis methods remains open.

**Physical-coordinate test.** The exact code uses EighteenReadData edge labels and FaceTurns.cornerNames = UFR,URB,ULF,UBL,DRF,DBR,DFL,DLB with cyclic shift j as the charge. For one selected sticker per distinct cubie, all 4096 edge choice assignments and 6561 corner choice assignments were counted. Edge sectors 0/1 have 867/861 histograms. Corner unrestricted support 795, residues 0/1/2 have 609/660/660 histograms. Test every ordered pair and surplus coordinate for the minimum number of simultaneous monotone unit transfers that preserve BOTH endpoints' residue feasibility. Edge legal residue zero: 1,631,310 obligations require one, 105,468 two (1,736,778 total). Odd: 1,597,338 one, 116,448 two. Corner legal residue zero: 767,352 one, 62,292 two, 2,760 three (832,404 total); each nonzero residue 945,084 one, 34,200 two, 384 three. The bounds 2 and 3 are attained in these actual histogram supports. In URFDLB order a corner witness is x=(4,0,0,4,0,0), y=(4,0,1,1,1,1), excess D; no symmetric monotone exchange using one or two transfers works. These are exact histograms in the local orientation-choice model with the physical twist convention, not a claim about full physical-state query complexity or permutation coupling. No change to the 0.29 theorem.

### Target-relative observation complexity: common permutation sign

The physical group is the fiber product of the orientation-sum-zero corner and edge wreath-product factors over permutation sign (Mereb & Vendramin, *Rubik's as Galois': The inverse Galois problem for the Rubik's Cube group*, §2.2, arXiv:2411.11566v3). We use the standard sufficiency of the three legality conditions; no new Lean proof of that sufficiency is claimed here. Consequently, compatible edge and corner posteriors combine freely subject to equality of their signs.

**Certificate minima (state-dependent addresses).** For either factor, the minimum number of single-sticker observations of a specified arrangement that forces its permutation sign is independent of the arrangement. Reindex the positions by actual cubie labels and cyclically shift the local coordinates by the actual twists; the sum of those shifts is zero, and reindexing only changes sign by a constant. This bijection maps the actual state to the solved reference, preserves the allowed sticker subsets and transports opposite-sign witnesses. Two distinct local sticker colors in their known cyclic order identify an oriented corner (checked for either relative offset); two edge stickers identify an oriented edge. Hence three corner reads on the same cubie are redundant. A certificate cannot leave two blank pieces (swap), two single-read pieces reporting the same color (exchange the labels and carry orientation with the necessary local-side offset; the offsets cancel), or a single-read piece whose reported color occurs on the sole blank piece (swap and choose the blank orientation to preserve the sum). The remaining patterns have at most one blank and distinct singleton colors, disjoint from the blank's colors. Enumerate these reduced patterns on the solved reference, and for each enumerate residual label assignments and compatible orientations, searching for an opposite-sign, residue-zero completion. All rejected patterns have explicit swap obstructions above. Results: 14891 edge patterns, 14109 sign certificates, minimum 18; 5873 corner patterns, 4389 certificates, minimum 11; all 464 cost-10 corner patterns fail. Witnesses and full counts in the registered audit. These are certificate sizes, NOT minimax query depths.

**Fixed sign depth.** For a factor of n pieces, 2(n-1) fixed observations suffice by reading two stickers on n-1 pieces, leaving the last identity forced. Fewer than 2(n-1) reads leave either two blank pieces, a blank and a singleton, or two singletons. Since the unknown arrangement is unrestricted subject to its orientation sum, place two labels sharing a color in those positions, arrange the relevant singleton readings to show that shared color and use another position to correct the total orientation residue if needed. The swap obstructions give two compatible arrangements of opposite signs. This proves fixed edge depth 22, corner depth 14 (for the actual n=12,8 factors). Combined paired fixed sign depth is 14: below 14 both factor queries have ambiguous sign histories, and the fiber product combines these into two indistinguishable physical states.

**Exact adaptive corner-sign recursion.** State = sorted multiset R of singly observed colors, remaining label mask L (one label per unresolved position), residual twist sum r modulo 3. There are |L|-|R| blank positions. A singly observed position is normalized so its first observed sticker is local side 0. Full observations identify label and normalized twist, then remove that row and label. Sorting/reindexing changes only the known permutation-sign offset, which is irrelevant to whether both signs occur. The controller retains the actual row/label mapping for recovering the absolute sign. In a state, the two-bit sign support is computed by recursively assigning one label containing the first color to that row; its required twist is the cyclic index of the color, and the inversion contribution is the number of unused smaller labels. With only blank rows left the support is both signs if at least two labels remain, singleton if one remains, and at zero labels feasible only when r=0. Blank twists can realize every residual residue. A state with singleton sign support is terminal. Otherwise every informative action is (i) read a blank row on any local side j=0,1,2, append the reported color and update r to r+j mod 3; (ii) read either of the other two sides of a singleton row, each of which identifies its label and twist, remove the label and subtract the normalized twist. Enumerate only feasible replies using the sign-support recurrence. Repeated reads and third reads add no information. The Bellman value is 1+min_action max_feasible_reply child value. The remaining read capacity decreases at every transition, so exact recursion is finite; quotienting identical rows and retaining r preserves all actions up to a known permutation-sign gauge. With alpha pruning of actions whose worst observed branch already meets the incumbent, audit returns 13 for the root and for each of the three first-side choices, 107472 cached ambiguous depth states, 218370 support states. Separately, seven fixed first reads give 642 realizable sorted prefixes and restricted continuation depths 4:48, 5:132, 6:462. Thus a simple seven-first policy also attains 13; full minimax excludes 12. This is a computer-assisted exact result for the stated recurrence, not kernel-verified.

**Physical paired sign transfer.** Upper bound: run the corner policy and ignore edge replies. Lower bound: suppose a paired policy of at most 12 reads identifies the common sign. Simulate it on one fixed residue-zero edge arrangement while allowing all residue-zero corner arrangements; every prefix of the fixed edge replies has both sign possibilities by the 18-read per-state edge certificate lower bound. For every corner trajectory, each compatible corner sign can therefore be completed to a legal paired state with the same transcript, by the fiber-product property. Thus the simulated policy must itself identify the corner sign within 12, contradicting the corner minimax result. Joint address realizability and free legal turns are the existing physical-model assumptions. Hence the paired common-sign optimum is 13, compared with fixed 14 and full reconstruction 19. This does not prove an improved full reconstruction policy or a new 0.29 axiom dependency. Independent recurrence audit and formalization are open.

### Corner objectives and the actual parity clock (0.30 follow-up)

The unpruned three-objective recurrence in auditCornerGoals independently recomputes the normalized posterior counts, capped at two per permutation-sign sector. The three stopping predicates are respectively one nonempty sign sector, exactly one candidate, and at most one candidate per sign sector (the sign is supplied at termination, NOT before choosing queries). The same feasible-action quotient from the sign audit applies to all three predicates. Exact root values are (13,13,12); fixed corner full-state depth is 14 by reading two stickers of seven pieces and the fixed-sign lower bound. The 13-read sign-optimal and full-state-optimal costs coincide; a full-state-optimal policy necessarily determines sign by 13 as well. This is not a six-read saving within the corner problem; 19 is the *paired full-cube* optimum. On 142217 cached non-singleton feasible states the histogram of (full depth minus sign depth, full depth minus terminal-sign depth) is (0,0):7857, (0,1):123118, (1,0):398, (1,1):10844. These are normalized state counts, not probabilities or frequencies of physical policy visits. No pruning of actions or replies in the depth recurrence; 256825 cached count states. A different capped-count calculation supports the earlier result, but uses the same quotient; independent raw 8-corner verification and Lean proof remain open.

For an **early** sign oracle, retain the required residual sign s in the same quotient state and restrict every child to the transported sign sector. When inserting the first color c of the first blank row into sorted R, use stable insertion after equal colors: the residual sign bit changes by the number of existing colors strictly greater than c, modulo 2. When resolving singleton row i to label j (rank k in the remaining label set), the residual sign bit changes by (i+k) mod 2 by minor expansion of the permutation matrix. These changes accompany the existing twist-residue updates. The unpruned early-sign recursion gives 12 for both initial signs, 253016 nonterminal sector-depth states and 256825 capped-count states. Thus D_early=D_late=12 < D_unknown=13 for the isolated corner factor; a free terminal sign suffices to capture the entire one-read benefit of a free initial sign. The early result is a finite computation on the quotient, not yet independently reimplemented or kernel-verified. It does not assert that the paired controller has access to a free sign oracle; the physical edge certificate lower bound prevents the edge channel alone from supplying that sign by frame 12.

The edge certificate lower bound 18 also follows directly without enumeration: two blank pieces permit a sign-reversing swap; if no blank exists, fewer than 18 reads leave at least seven singleton pieces, so two report the same one of six colors and can exchange with zero net flip change. With one blank, fewer than 18 reads leave at least five singletons; their colors must be distinct and absent from the blank label, which leaves only four available colors. The same swap obstructions establish impossibility. The previous enumeration supplies an attaining example. This removes the computational premise from the paired sign lower bound's edge-side obstruction.

The original NineteenSchedule.lean clock fully observes six corners in frames 1..12, observes one side of a seventh in frame 13, repeats a known address in frames 14..18, then resolves the seventh at frame 19. With two entirely blank corners sign is ambiguous by a swap. At frame 13 the two remaining labels form an unordered pair, and the singly observed color disambiguates them iff it belongs to only one of the pair. The blank twist can absorb the residue, hence sign uniqueness and full corner uniqueness coincide at this prefix. Among the 28 unordered corner pairs, 4 share zero colors, 12 share one, 12 share two (binary cube vertex distance 3,2,1). For a uniform legal cube the first observed color of the remaining singleton is uniform among its three colors conditional on the remaining labels; the ambiguous fraction is (12+24)/(28*3)=3/7, hence fraction 4/7 has determined sign and full corner at frame 13, the rest at frame 19. Repeats 14..18 change nothing in the corner-only posterior. These are corner-only marginal probabilities, not joint sign activation probabilities after edge filtering.

As the frozen edge policy ignores corner replies during its queries and only uses the terminal sign, an alternate clock that moves the last new corner read to frame 14 (and pads later), or an adaptive full-corner policy completing by 13, can be paired with the same edge decision tree under joint address realizability without increasing the 19-frame upper bound. This is a mathematical alternative controller argument, not a modification of the frozen certificate, a new Lean theorem, or an improvement to the already exact 19 worst-case cost. Prioritize actual joint stopping-time and expected-cost analysis only if it improves a stated objective; do not claim earlier sign alone reduces 19.

### Charged cycles and a signed-graphic matroid formula for edge certificates

Normalize a reference compatible arrangement by reindexing positions by labels and subtracting its local twists (sum zero). For every position i and label j, insert an arc i->j for each compatible relative orientation t; nonzero-twist loops are allowed. Every alternative arrangement is a vertex-disjoint union of directed simple cycles, with remaining positions unchanged. Its relative charge in G=C2 x C_m is the sum of cycle charges ((length-1) mod 2, sum of relative twists mod m). An opposite-sign zero-residue completion exists exactly when disjoint cycles sum to g=(1,0). A minimal such collection has no nonempty zero-sum subcollection, and no proper subcollection summing to g. Appending -g gives a minimal zero-sum sequence; hence at most D(G)-1 cycles suffice (2 for edges, 5 for corners in the general model). This is a structural existence bound, not a claim that finding these cycles is efficient in arbitrary graphs.

The charged-cycle audit compares with direct assignment enumeration over all previously reduced solved-reference certificate patterns. Edge: 14891 patterns, 782 ambiguous (all minimum one charged cycle). Corner: 5873 patterns, 1484 ambiguous (1172 minimum one, 312 minimum two). Witness with singly observed UBL[1], DRF[2], DBR[2], DFL[0], DLB[1], all others fully read: exactly one opposite-sign residue-zero completion; cycles (UBL,DLB) with relative twists (1,1), charge (1,2), and (DRF,DFL,DBR) with relative twists (2,0,2), charge (0,1). Neither cycle alone preserves residue and reverses sign. The finite census applies to reduced patterns, not raw masks or policy paths.

**General graph certificate theorem.** Let H be a nonempty finite simple graph with n edges, v nonisolated vertices and b nontrivial bipartite connected components. Each edge is a distinct two-color cubie; arrangements permute all n cubies and flip them with total flip zero. Each query reads one chosen sticker. The minimum sign certificate at every arrangement is

    C_sign(H) = 2n - v + max(0,b-1).

This is the single-factor, state-dependent certificate cost, not adaptive minimax cost. Proof: normalize to solved reference as above. Two blank positions, two singleton observations with equal color, or a singleton whose color belongs to the sole blank label allow a sign-reversing sum-preserving swap. Thus any certificate has at most one blank, distinct singleton colors, and excludes both blank colors from singletons. Direct each singleton edge from observed color to its other endpoint. This gives a partial functional digraph (outdegree <=1). With a blank, its label cannot move into any singleton row, so its position is fixed in every completion and its flip is forced by the sum. On nonblank singletons, a nontrivial permutation cycle is precisely a directed color cycle: each label entering a different row must show its other color, and the row-side offsets telescope, giving relative flip sum equal to cycle length modulo 2. An even directed cycle is therefore exactly an opposite-sign, flip-preserving obstruction; every odd cycle has even permutation sign. Hence reduced records are certificates iff their selected singleton graph has no even directed cycle. This includes the zero-singleton case.

The selected undirected singleton edges form a pseudoforest: distinct source colors imply outdegree <=1, hence each component has at most one cycle, and any cycle must be consistently directed. For a certificate each such cycle must be odd. Conversely any pseudoforest of this type can be directed with distinct sources (toward a root in a tree, toward and around the unique cycle otherwise). In the no-blank case these are precisely the independent edge sets of the all-negative signed-graphic frame matroid, classical rank v-b (Zaslavsky 1982, Thm 5.1(c), Cor 7D.3(g),(j), DOI 10.1016/0166-218X(82)90033-6). Thus minimum no-blank cost is 2n-v+b. With one blank edge, its component has at most v_component-2 singleton sources, while every other bipartite component loses at least one source because a functional graph on all its vertices would contain an even directed cycle. Attainment: in blank component use a spanning tree containing the blank edge, remove that edge, and direct the resulting two trees toward its endpoints; elsewhere use rooted spanning trees for bipartite components, odd unicyclic spanning subgraphs for nonbipartite components. Optimize blank location: if b>0 put blank in a bipartite component, gaining one read over no blank; if b=0 no blank already attains bound. This proves the formula. Isolated vertices contribute nothing. The actual K(2,2,2) has n=12,v=6,b=0, yielding 18 with an analytic upper and lower; the signed-graphic rank is classical, the observation-certificate reduction is the result here, priority open. Direct enumeration on all 1094 nonempty labeled simple graphs of 2..5 vertices, 312453 reduced patterns (306669 certificates), agrees with even-cycle criterion and formula. This does not establish the general adaptive minimax depth, or modify the accepted 0.29 result.

### Full-state certificates versus sign certificates

In the same nonempty simple-graph model, let c be the number of nontrivial connected components. Then

    C_full(H) = 2n - v + c - 1.

The swap obstructions used for sign remain necessary for full recovery. For reduced records with no blank, each directed singleton cycle can be independently reversed; an even cycle has relative flip zero and changes the permutation, while each odd cycle has relative flip one. Therefore uniqueness is equivalent to no even cycle and at most one odd cycle in the entire singleton graph. With a blank, its orientation can absorb any odd-cycle flip, so no directed cycle is allowed. In the no-blank case the maximum singleton count is v-c+1 if H has a nonbipartite component, otherwise v-c: take rooted spanning trees and at most one odd unicyclic component. With one blank, singleton edges must form a forest whose sources avoid both blank colors. The component containing the blank admits at most v_component-2 sources; all others at most v_component-1. Equality is realized by a spanning tree containing the blank, deleting that edge and rooting at both endpoints, together with rooted trees in the other components. This saves v-c+1 reads relative to reading both sides everywhere, so achieves the formula in all cases.

For sign versus full-state certificates the difference is c-1-max(0,b-1). In particular every connected graph has equal minimum certificate sizes, whether bipartite or not; disconnected nonbipartite components can separate the objectives. At the reduced singleton-edge level, the no-blank full-state condition is the independent-set condition of the all-negative signed-graph lift matroid (at most one unbalanced cycle globally and no balanced cycles), in contrast with the frame matroid allowing one unbalanced cycle per component. This is a structural correspondence with known matroids, not a new matroid definition. The 1094-graph audit over 312453 reduced patterns directly enumerates legal completions capped at two and confirms the full-state criterion and formula, 305389 certificates; 1084 graphs have equal costs and 10 have a one-read gap. For K(2,2,2) both minima are 18; the physical paired 19-frame minimax theorem is a distinct statement. The audit does not settle adaptive minimax for arbitrary graph families.

### Adaptive observation lower bound from vertex covers

For the same nonempty simple color graph H with n distinct two-color pieces and total flip zero, let tau be the minimum vertex cover size, and tau* the minimum size of a vertex cover containing both endpoints of at least one edge. Then tau <= tau* <= tau+1 and adaptive sign identification depth is at least 2n-tau*. Proof: fix such a cover S with an internal edge e. Against any deterministic adaptive policy, assign an unused label to each newly touched position, reserving e until the last first read. For every first read return an endpoint in S by choosing the orientation of the assigned label. A second read is forced by that choice. When the last untouched position is first queried, assign e and choose its orientation to make the total flip zero; either answer belongs to S. If a transcript stops before all positions have been touched, the untouched orientations can absorb the required flip. Consequently the entire adversarial transcript is realizable. A sign certificate cannot have two blank positions, duplicate singleton colors, or a singleton color shared by the sole blank label (the sign-reversing swaps proved above). With no blank there are at most |S| singleton positions. With one blank its reserved label e has both colors in S, so at most |S|-2 singleton positions. In either case at least 2n-|S| distinct reads are needed. Repeated reads cannot help. Taking a minimum cover containing an edge proves the bound. The weaker 2n-tau-1 follows by adding one endpoint to a minimum independent cover when necessary. Full-state depth inherits the sign bound. K(2,2,2) has tau=tau*=4, hence at least 20 reads, strictly above its state-dependent 18-read certificate. This is the isolated edge factor with either permutation sign allowed, not the physical paired model or a known-sign lower bound.

**Symmetry reduction.** For a graph automorphism pi, transport the remaining label set L by its induced edge permutation and the singleton-color multiset R by pi. In the normalized side-zero convention, let epsilon_pi(e) be one when the transported ordered endpoints of label e are opposite to its canonical endpoint order. The residual flip bit becomes r XOR sum_{e in L} epsilon_pi(e); the sum is independent of the candidate arrangement since every remaining label occurs exactly once. Reordering the rows and remaining label order changes permutation parity by a fixed offset. Therefore sign constancy, full uniqueness and per-sign uniqueness are invariant, and each first/second observation is transported bijectively to an observation with the same cost. This is an exact quotient for these three objectives. When a blank remains its local coordinate gauge makes the residual flip bit irrelevant. For K5, all 120 color permutations act. A second, unpruned implementation uses direct residual assignment DFS for capped counts and memoizes the entire symmetry orbit of each computed depth state; it gives (17,17,16), 2162 evaluated depth states covering exactly 175226 nonterminal depth states, and 17398 count-oracle calls. The two algorithms use the same normalized-state model; this is a distinct implementation and additional symmetry validation, not a blind independent physical-state oracle or kernel proof.

**Edge-partition lower bound.** For any partition E(H)=E_1 disjoint union ... disjoint union E_k into nonempty edge sets, regard H_i as the corresponding color graphs (vertices may overlap). Then

    D_sign(H) >= sum_i D_sign(H_i),   D_full(H) >= sum_i D_full(H_i).

Restrict the input to disjoint known position blocks occupied by their designated labels, with total flip zero in each block. Every product of local states is a valid global state. Global permutation sign is the XOR of local signs up to a known constant. Even giving the block membership free cannot avoid identifying every local sign, since each unresolved local sign can be changed independently while preserving all other replies. For each local belief maintain its optimal remaining sign depth d_i. Every local query has a feasible response with new d_i >= old d_i-1, by the minimax recurrence; other d_j do not change. Until the sum of depths is zero, the product sign remains ambiguous. The adversary therefore needs at least the sum of the initial depths. The same potential proof applies to full-state targets. On the restricted product problem the corresponding upper bound is obtained by running each local policy in sequence. The direct-sum principle is standard decision-tree complexity (Jain, Klauck & Santha 2010, arXiv:1004.0105, Thm 1 concerns separate outputs; the XOR version here follows from independence and ambiguity), not claimed new. The application to edge partitions and the explicit graph family below are the structural consequences.

For each k>=1, construct a connected simple graph H_k with k disjoint K5 blocks and one new hub joined to one specified vertex of each block. Then n=11k, v=5k+1, tau*=4k: each K5 requires four cover vertices, and selecting four in each block including the hub neighbor covers all edges without the hub and contains internal edges. Partition into the k K5 edge sets and the k-edge star. The star has sign depth exactly 2k-2 (cover lower tau*=2, fixed observation upper; also k=1 gives zero). Therefore

    D_sign(H_k) >= 17k + (2k-2) = 19k-2,
    2n-tau*(H_k) = 18k.

For k>=3 the cover lower bound underestimates sign depth by at least k-2; hence its error is unbounded even on connected simple graphs. This lower bound relies on the finite K5 computation, not an analytic proof of K5 depth17 or an exact formula for H_k. The static certificate formula gives C_sign=C_full=17k-1 for H_k, so the adaptive-versus-certificate gap is at least 2k-1. No new physical-cube optimum is inferred. A stronger general lower bound is the maximum over edge partitions of the sums of known local depths, together with the cover bound; optimizing it is separate from proving an exact minimax formula.

### Hypergraph transversal adversary: analytic corner lower bounds

Consider n labeled pieces, each with m>=2 distinct colors in a fixed cyclic order. A state is a permutation of the pieces and cyclic shifts with total shift zero in C_m. Queries return the color at a specified position and local side; charge one per query. Let tau be the minimum size of a set S of colors meeting every piece's color set (hypergraph transversal number). For the isolated factor with either permutation sign allowed,

    D_sign >= max(0, 2n-tau-1).

If S also contains the full color set of some piece e, then D_sign >= max(0, 2n-|S|). Full-state identification without a sign oracle inherits both bounds. The latter recovers the preceding graph tau* bound for m=2. These are lower bounds even when two reads do not identify an oriented piece.

Proof: on first touching any position, assign an unused piece and choose its cyclic shift to report a color in S, except on the last first touch, when its shift is chosen to satisfy the total shift equation (the last color may be outside S). All further replies on touched positions are forced. If the policy stops with untouched positions, their shifts can satisfy the total equation. Two untouched positions permit a permutation transposition preserving the shift sum. Two singly observed positions reporting the same color also permit a transposition: if label j has that color at cyclic index p_j(c) and the observed side is s_i, the required shift is p_j(c)-s_i, so swapping the two labels preserves the sum. With exactly one blank, any singleton color in its label allows a transposition with that blank, whose shift absorbs the difference. Hence sign determination requires at most one blank, distinct singleton colors, and no singleton color belonging to the blank's label. If all touched there are at most tau+1 singletons; if one blank there are at most tau-1 because its label meets S. Counting at least two observations per nonsingleton touched position proves 2n-tau-1 in both cases. For the stronger bound reserve e until the last first touch; all first replies now lie in S, and if one blank, at least m>=2 colors of S are unavailable to singletons. Thus at least 2n-|S| reads in either case.

**Fixed-sign full-state lower bound.** In the same model, for either initially prescribed permutation sign (when the sector is nonempty),

    D_early >= max(0, 2n-tau-2).

This is also a lower bound for D_late. For n>=2 the preceding adversary can arrange the required sign by deciding which of the last two unassigned labels goes into the penultimate first-touched position; either label meets S. If the controller stops with >=2 blanks, choose the remaining permutation to meet the sign and vary two blank shifts by opposite nonzero residues, preserving all observations, sign and total shift. If all positions touched in at most 2n-tau-3 queries, at least tau+3 singleton positions use at most tau+1 colors. There is a triple of equal colors or two disjoint equal-color pairs. Cycle the three labels, or transpose both pairs, preserving sign and shift sum by the same formula. If one blank, at least tau+1 singleton positions use colors in S. Add a fictitious observation of some color of the blank label belonging to S, at the side where it appears in the adversarial completion. There are now >=tau+2 singly read positions using <=tau colors, so the same even-permutation collision exists, preserving even the fictitious observation. It is also a collision for the real transcript. n=1 has trivial nonnegative bound. The argument requires unrestricted permutations within the prescribed sign and a global cyclic shift equation, not arbitrary physical move restrictions.

For actual corners, the eight labels consist of all triples choosing one color from each of three opposite pairs. Any opposite pair is a transversal of size two, and no one color meets all labels. Thus tau=2, n=8, giving D_sign>=13 and D_early>=12 analytically, and D_full>=13, D_late>=12. Combined with the previously computed upper policies, the exact isolated corner values (sign/full/early/late)=(13,13,12,12) now have entirely analytic lower bounds. Upper existence still uses finite-state computation, not an independently kernel-checked construction. Together with the edge certificate minimum18 and the earlier physical fiber-product simulation, this supplies an analytic 13-frame lower for the physical common-sign target. The original paired full19 theorem remains unchanged.

### Hall-tight schedules replace the corner upper minimax search

Read one fixed side on seven distinct corners, leaving exactly one blank. Because the blank absorbs the residual twist, the possible label assignments are precisely the injections of the seven singleton rows into the eight corner labels with each assigned label containing its reported color. For a feasible residual instance with k rows and k+1 labels, a nonempty row subset T is Hall-tight when |N(T)|=|T|. Every matching uses all labels in N(T) for T. Resolve all but one row in T and all other singleton rows; the omitted row is then forced, as is the blank label and its twist. Thus a tight set permits completion in k-1 further reads. If k>=2 and a terminal sign is supplied, resolve all but two rows in T if |T|>=2, otherwise omit the forced singleton of T and one other row. The remaining possibilities are either unique or differ by a single transposition, so sign selects the unique completion in k-2 further reads. The latter construction does not use the sign during queries and hence also works when sign is supplied initially.

The 642 feasible seven-first color multisets form 29 orbits under the 48 permutations of the three opposite pairs and their members. These symmetries are used only for color incidence; actual cyclic orientations are decoded in original coordinates. The frozen audit table gives a fixed order of singleton colors to resolve for each representative. Stop following the order as soon as a tight set occurs; otherwise proceed to the next color. All orders have length <=5 and reach a tight set on every feasible assignment. The verifier directly enumerates injections instead of computing optimal depths or trusting the schedule search: 8408 injections over 29 representatives, 25516 positive path checks, 8408 terminal-sign checks (48 unique candidates, 8360 two candidates of opposite sign). The 642 multiset coverage by required maximum preliminary order length 0..5 is [258,12,60,156,126,30]. Shortening the order for representative 0001125 from 00011 to 0001 is rejected. Negative-control statistics are excluded from the positive census. Seven first reads plus six or five second reads prove full/sign<=13 and late/early<=12 respectively. **General Hall-block packing lemma.** For any feasible one-blank incidence instance as above, let M be a matching of all k rows, with unmatched label b. Form a directed graph on rows and a special blank vertex: i->j iff row i admits label M(j), and i->blank iff i admits b; blank has no outgoing arc to a row. T is tight iff T is nonempty, excludes blank and is closed under outgoing arcs, because M(T) is contained in N(T) and equality holds precisely when no other label is admitted. In particular a tight set exists iff the rank-k transversal matroid on the k+1 labels has a coloop: each label in a tight neighborhood is mandatory, and deleting a mandatory label violates Hall on some previously tight subset. The maximum number t of disjoint nonempty tight sets equals the number of sink SCCs excluding blank. Every nonempty out-closed set contains a sink SCC; conversely those SCCs themselves are disjoint tight sets. This gives a linear-time SCC computation after a matching is chosen, and the number is independent of that choice. Disjoint tight sets have disjoint neighborhoods, so omitting one row per sink SCC saves t further observations: full recovery needs at most k-t reads. If t<k and t>0, one further omission is possible with terminal sign: choose two omitted rows in one block of size>=2, or omit one additional row outside the blocks; all remaining ambiguity is one transposition. If t=k, all labels are already forced. The bounds are sufficient, not a universal optimal adaptive characterization. The audit enumerates 18208 feasible sorted one-blank residual incidence states of the actual corner labels, compares exhaustive disjoint tight-set packing with SCC counts, and obtains histogram t=0:7078,1:10498,2:624,3:8, with no discrepancy. The SCC characterization is a structural restatement of classical matching decomposition, not claimed as a new matroid theorem. No change to the physical paired 19-frame theorem or its proof.

### Exact signed terminal structure for graph edge observations

Given any feasible residual record, choose a compatible flip-zero reference arrangement, remove the fully resolved pieces, and reindex the remaining positions by the reference labels. Each singleton edge is directed from its observed color toward its other color; a blank edge, if present, is kept separately. The following criterion tests **simultaneous at-most-one completion in each sign sector**. It is not a claim that a specified early sign sector cannot be unique in some other cases. Two or more blanks always violate simultaneous per-sign uniqueness, since opposite nonzero flips of two blank edges preserve the sign and flip sum.

Let f_c be the number of singleton rows showing color c. For each fixed choice of observed endpoint on each nonblank label (and, when present, of the unused blank label), the number of assignments to the singleton rows is F=product_c(f_c!). The orientation of a singleton is then determined by its row side, and the blank's flip is uniquely determined by the sum. For zero blanks, restrict endpoint choices to the required flip residue: row-side offsets telescope in the total. If any f_c>=2, swapping two rows of color c preserves orientation sum and reverses sign; hence the assignments for each endpoint choice split equally between signs. Consequently F>2 rules out per-sign uniqueness; F=2 requires a unique feasible endpoint-choice pattern. F=1 means all observed colors are distinct.

* Zero blanks, F=2: per-sign uniqueness iff the reference directed color graph contains neither an even directed simple cycle nor two edge-disjoint odd directed simple cycles. Indeed the set of differently selected endpoints between two patterns is balanced, hence decomposes into edge-disjoint directed cycles; equal flip residue requires an even total number of reversed edges. Conversely reversing an even cycle or two disjoint odd cycles preserves the residue. With F=2 each extra feasible pattern supplies another candidate of both signs.
* Zero blanks, F=1: the directed graph has outdegree at most one, so its cycles are vertex-disjoint. Reversing a length-l cycle changes permutation sign by (l-1) mod2 and flip by l mod2. All alternative patterns are subsets of those cycles. The per-sign uniqueness criterion is at most one even cycle and at most one odd cycle: two even cycles or two odd cycles yield a nonempty sign-zero, flip-zero alternative; otherwise the only possible nontrivial residue-zero reversal is the single even cycle, which reverses sign.
* One blank, F=2: per-sign uniqueness iff neither endpoint of the blank is a singleton color and there is no directed cycle. If the blank shares a singleton color, exchanging its label with that row creates a distinct endpoint-choice pattern, and the blank absorbs the required flip. If it shares none, its position is forced and endpoint patterns differ only by balanced cycle reversals, with any cycle permitted because of the blank's flip.
* One blank, F=1: if neither blank endpoint is a singleton color, per-sign uniqueness iff there is no directed cycle or exactly one directed cycle of even length. All cycles are vertex-disjoint and the blank absorbs the flip. If both blank endpoints are singleton colors, the reference and two separate blank exchanges give at least three candidates. If exactly one is a singleton color c, per-sign uniqueness iff the other endpoint of the reference singleton edge at c is not a singleton color and there is no directed cycle. To see this, contract each reference row and label. A permutation cycle involving the blank is a path starting at the blank, following its possible exchange with the singleton at c and continuing through successive other endpoints of reference singleton edges. If the path can take a second step, the reference, first exchange and two-step exchange give at least three assignments. If it cannot, the only blank-moving cycle is the transposition; any directed cycle elsewhere would supply an additional assignment. With no such cycle the reference and that transposition have opposite signs and are the only assignments.

These are exact local criteria for any finite simple two-color graph with the stated global flip and sign constraints; they require a feasible reference but not a full count of legal completions. For an exact 19-distinct-read record on the cube, the only potentially successful residual shapes are five singletons/no blank or three singletons/one blank (two blanks are impossible for uniqueness). The reference-color graph is particularly small, so the criteria specialize directly. This is an endpoint-color reformulation of the earlier charged-cycle matching criterion, not a new adaptive upper/lower bound or a novelty claim.
